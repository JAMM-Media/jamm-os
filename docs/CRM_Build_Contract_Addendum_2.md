# JAMM PX CRM Build Contract, Addendum 2
## Pricing Depth, the Form Contract, and Why the Foundation Got Stronger
August 13, 2026. Author: Andrew. Supplements the CRM Build Contract and Addendum 1, both dated August 12, 2026. Where those documents disagree with this one, this one governs. Reading time: about ten minutes. The Build Reference v2 (separate document) has the full system with reasoning if you want the deep version; this addendum covers everything you need for your build.

## 1. What changed, in one sentence
Addendum 1 section 3 capped complexity at two layers (a categorical answer opening one numeric sub-question, never deeper); that cap is removed, and firms can now chain pricing dimensions to arbitrary depth, with the schema for it already shipped on my track as of August 13.
## 2. What you actually need to change in your build: almost nothing, and here is the list
Delete the depth-two assumption from your mental model and any code shaped by it. There is no "one sub-question maximum" anymore.
Do not build any within-flag branching on numeric answers. When a flag applies to a lead, the firm's configured dimensions for that flag are ALL served as ordinary questions by the config endpoint, regardless of how the firm chained them underneath for pricing. The chain shapes pricing, not question visibility, and the form never sees it. If you were planning conditional reveal of the volume question based on the type answer, don't; the endpoint serves the question list flat and your form asks what it serves.
Payload contract is unchanged. Facts only: flag keys, canonical option IDs, raw numbers, free text only in Other fields. Numeric questions capture the raw number; my server maps it to the firm's tiers at resolution time.
Everything else in Addendum 1 stands as written: the projection principle, one question per screen, answer buttons writing the same fields as form answers, the post-call gate, reply pausing, the accept toggle, the Concierge onboarding checklist, all of it.
That is the complete tweak list. The rest of this document is understanding, not work.
## 3. The system you are building on, explained properly
The core idea from Addendum 1 has not moved: the intake form is a projection of firm configuration. Firms never edit the form; they configure their firm, and the form renders itself from a public config endpoint on my track. What changed is how much expressive power sits underneath that configuration, and it is worth you understanding it even though none of it reaches your code.
The two-sided split. The complexity catalog (the flags, the dimensions, the question wording, the answer vocabularies) is system-owned, global, shared by every firm. Firms attach prices to it; they never author lead-facing text. Everything financial (which services a firm offers, base fees, tiers, prices, guards) is firm-scoped with absolute tenant isolation, like everything else in the product. This split is why your form can be one codebase serving thousands of visually different forms: the questions are ours, the selection and depth of them is the firm's.
How a firm prices a flag now. Each dimension a firm configures gets a direction. Either it is a separate flat fee (its own tiers, its own prices, added to the total independently), or it is dependent (it hangs under a specific tier or option of a coarser dimension, and the firm prices the combinations directly, down to any depth). A firm might price crypto as "per account bracket" plus "per transaction bracket," two flat fees, summed. Another firm prices "2 accounts at 1k-20k transactions" as one number and "10 accounts at the same volume" as another. Both are now expressible, and the same firm will use different styles on different flags. The system owns the order dimensions may chain in (accounts can parent transactions, never the reverse), so every firm that chains a flag chains it the same direction.
Where the money resolves. Entirely on my backend, at proposal time: base fee, plus the categorical option adder, plus each chain walked through the lead's raw answers to a single number, plus each flat layer, with guards able to override everything to quote. Your build never computes fees client-side, exactly as Addendum 1 said.
## 4. Why this will not fail on you
This is the part I most want you to have, because your engine's automation quality depends on trusting the pricing layer under it.
The system can never produce a wrong price, only a computed one or a human one. The universal quote law got stronger, not weaker, with depth: any lead answer landing on anything the firm did not consciously price, at any level (an unpriced option, an unpriced tier, an unpriced cell deep in a chain, a number above the firm's ceiling, a crossed guard, the Other option) routes to needs-a-quote through one enforcement point. A firm's unfinished pricing corners are quote territory, not errors and never silent mispricing. So your automation's worst case is always "a human gets handed this lead with their answers attached," which is the designed behavior, not a failure mode.
Blank and zero are physically different in the schema. A blank price means "unpriced, route to quote." An explicit zero means "the firm charges nothing for this, deliberately." The old settings blob could not tell those apart; the new tables cannot confuse them, and tests guard the distinction including the realistic bug (defaulting blank to zero). For your engine that means a quote-routing decision is always a real firm decision, never an artifact.
Firms cannot build broken structures. Save-time validation enforces: tiers contiguous and complete per branch (no gaps, no overlaps), chains only flowing coarse-to-fine, prices only at the end of a chain so nothing double counts, and restructuring a firm's pricing being an explicit confirmed destructive action rather than something a routine edit can do by accident. All of it is guard-tested with negative controls (each test was deliberately broken and watched to fail before being trusted).
The data stays comparable for the intelligence layer. Because your form captures raw numbers and canonical option IDs rather than tier labels, every lead's real quantities land in the behavioral log in identical shape no matter how differently two firms cut their pricing. Nothing about firm-side depth fragments the cross-firm data your sensor exists to collect.
Simple firms never see the machinery. The system-shipped defaults never nest. A firm that wants two flat fees sets two flat fees. Depth is opt-in, one added tier at a time, in the pricing settings UI (which I am building, along with the template frontend, to keep your queue clear).
## 5. Sequencing
The public config endpoint is my next session and remains the thing your form phase consumes; the endpoint contract in Addendum 1 section 9 is unchanged (active types with labels, questions with system-written text, options with opaque IDs, numeric questions per the firm's unit; never prices, adders, guards, boundaries, or structure). Your phase order does not change. Step zero (v2 ML spec confirm) still stands.
